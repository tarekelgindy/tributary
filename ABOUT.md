# About — why Tributary exists

*This page is the mission. The [README](README.md) and [INTRO](INTRO.md) speak tool — what Tributary computes, what it costs, how to run it. This is about why it's worth computing at all.*

## The problem underneath the problem

Two structural facts about the modern information environment do most of the damage usually blamed on "misinformation":

1. **Provenance is invisible.** By the time a narrative reaches a feed it has been stripped of its history — no first attestation, no amplification path, no record of how it mutated on the way. An organic concern that has circulated for decades and a talking point seeded last Tuesday arrive looking identical. Without provenance, every judgment about a claim collapses into a judgment about the person sharing it.

2. **Engagement curation buries agreement.** Feeds optimize for reaction, and the most reliable reaction is outrage at another circle's worst take. The overlap between circles — the framings they share, the facts they both accept — is the least clickable thing the system produces, so it is systematically the least visible. The result is a perception gap: people don't just disagree with the other side, they carry a wildly inaccurate picture of what the other side actually believes. Correcting perception gaps is one of the few depolarization interventions with real evidence behind it; pure "exposure to the other side" is not.

Tributary's loop aims at exactly these two failures. Provenance makes narrative history visible; framing overlap makes buried agreement visible. And each half protects the other: provenance keeps overlap honest (organic concern and seeded talking point are structurally distinguishable, so "both circles say X" never has to mean false equivalence), and overlap keeps provenance humane (a trace read against what the circles actually share is context; read alone, it's tribal ammunition).

## The wager (theory of change)

Not mass persuasion. No tool is going to argue the public out of its bubbles, and Tributary doesn't try. The wager is narrower: make provenance and overlap **cheap** — so cheap that the people who already do this work by hand can afford to do it at scale. Journalists tracing a rumor for hours per claim. Educators building media-literacy exercises. Bridging organizations hunting for defensible common ground. Researchers measuring spread. These multipliers are the audience; their audiences are the reach.

**Shared methods, not shared arbiters.** The product is never "trust us." It is a receipt trail — dated attestations, verified quotes, a published error rate — that a hostile reader can check and still be forced to engage with.

## What success looks like

The north-star metric is **cross-line travel**: an artifact shared by accounts from more than one cluster. Not traffic. A trace that circulates in only one bubble is functioning as a weapon no matter how accurate it is; one that travels across lines is functioning as a bridge. Every gate in [MISSION_PLAN.md](MISSION_PLAN.md) after the first is deliberately a fact about *other people's behavior* — evidence that someone outside the bubble this tool was built in finds it fair and useful.

## The honesty rule (non-negotiable)

Tributary is allowed to report **low** overlap. If a week's data shows the circles share almost nothing, that finding gets published like any other. A tool that always finds heartwarming common ground is harmony propaganda — and propaganda for a good cause is still propaganda. Conclusion-neutral cuts both ways: it forbids "everything is seeded disinformation" and "deep down we all agree" alike.

## Further out (held lightly)

The distant ambition is personal rather than institutional: onramps and offramps — ways for an individual reader to see what their own information diet foregrounds and buries, and to step outside it voluntarily, without being told what to believe. That vision stays parked until the gates in front of it pass. The near-term work is the loop, done honestly, at multiplier scale.
