I build Tributary, an open instrument that answers two questions about any
story: where did this claim come from (who said it first, who amplified it,
how it mutated — every hop dated, linked, and mechanically verified), and how
is coverage of this event splitting (the competing framings, who carries each,
and the common ground every side accepts). No truth verdicts, ever — only
structural claims a reader can check, each with its receipt attached.

This week's Common Ground Report is a fair sample of what it does:
https://tarekelgindy.github.io/tributary/digests/issue-03.html
(One finding: of 294 stories both left- and right-rated outlets covered last
week, 95 never made anyone's top-of-feed — the agreement is real and it is
systematically the least visible thing in the feed.)

Three things that might matter to you specifically:

1. Everything has a stable permalink and public JSON — every trace, every
   event map, every share figure can be cited, embedded, or re-analyzed.
   Browse: https://tarekelgindy.github.io/tributary/corpus.html
2. The methodology and the corrections log are public, including our
   failures and their root causes. Readers can contribute corrections on any
   trace — named, reviewed, and credited, Wikipedia-style.
3. It runs itself: a capture instrument (36 feeds + news-sitemap censuses,
   every 6 hours since June) plus on-demand tracing from the site — a trace
   your desk requests exists as a citable page about ten minutes later.

If any of this is useful to your work, I'd genuinely value 20 minutes of

## B — "support it" (bridging orgs, depolarization funders)

Subject: Measuring the common ground feeds bury — working instrument, not a deck

Hi <name>,

<One sentence referencing their specific program/grant focus.>

Tributary is a working open-source instrument built on one premise: the
perception gap is partly an information-supply problem. Engagement-ranked
feeds systematically hide what circles agree on — so we measure it. Every
week, unattended, it records what left- and right-rated outlets both covered
and neither front-paged. Last week: 294 shared stories, 95 buried — one in
three of everything both sides agreed was worth covering.

The current weekly report: https://tarekelgindy.github.io/tributary/digests/issue-03.html
How it works, including what it refuses to claim: https://github.com/tarekelgindy/tributary/blob/main/METHODOLOGY.md

What makes it different from a media-bias chart:

1. It is provenance-first: claims are traced to dated, linked origins, so
   "both sides say X" never becomes false equivalence — an organic decades-old
   concern and a freshly seeded talking point look identical in a feed but are
   structurally distinguishable in a trace.
2. It is radically corrigible: public methodology, public corrections log
   with root causes, per-element AI/human provenance labels, and a reader
   contribution flow where corrections publish credited after review.
3. It is cheap and already running: static site, ~$0.70 per on-demand trace,
   captures since June with zero missed days. Support buys scale and audit
   depth, not existence.

I'm a solo builder; what I need most is <ask: pilot partners / audit
collaborators / funding for the measured-error-rate program>. Happy to walk
through anything, including the failure log.
