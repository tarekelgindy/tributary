Common Ground Report #2 — This Week in Narratives, Issue #3

September 23 – 30, 2026 · from Tributary, an open instrument that traces where narratives come from, who amplifies them, and what different information ecosystems see.

The question this issue asks: what did the left and right media circles both cover this week that neither side's front feed showed you? "Circles" means news outlets AllSides rates left/lean-left or right/lean-right (their ratings, not ours). Every quote below is an outlet's own headline, in its own voice. Tributary never labels claims true or false. Methodology · corrections · suggest a correction

The week in one measurement

Our capture instrument (36 feeds + news-sitemap censuses, every 6 hours, running unattended since June) recorded 5,222 distinct stories this week. 294 were covered by member outlets of both circles. 199 of those made at least one circle's top-of-feed. The remaining 95 stories — one in three of everything the circles agreed was worth covering — never surfaced at the top of anyone's feed on either side.

That is this issue's finding. Last issue, the common ground was old framings both sides carry (three traced origins); this week we analyzed two of the most-shared stories at framing level and found no confirmed cross-circle framing intersection — the rated circles were too thin on each (that negative is reported, not hidden; the tool is allowed to find low overlap). This week, the agreement lived one level down: in the stories themselves.

Where the week's biggest pocketbook story lived

Thirty-year mortgage rates crossed 7% for the first time since January 2025. We mapped the coverage: 8 competing framings, 36 distinct carriers — from Generational Affordability Crisis to Historical Normalization (“rates aren't exceptional”) to War-Driven Inflation Accountability. The structural finding: the carriers are overwhelmingly financial and trade press (Bloomberg, Bankrate, HousingWire, the Dallas Fed) plus advocacy shops on the left; not one right-of-center rated outlet appears in our sample. The biggest kitchen-table story of the week was told almost entirely outside the partisan spectrum — worth knowing before anyone calls it “ignored.” (Carrier counts are a floor from our search, not a census.) What we can't tell you

  • Whether any buried story was buried on purpose — “buried” here is a measurement (both circles demonstrably covered it; no member outlet ever surfaced it at feed position ≤5 in our captures), not an intent claim, and it says nothing about placement on outlets' own websites.
  • Whether the circles' framings of these stories overlap — this week's two framing-level analyses had too few rated-circle carriers to say (the thresholds behind coverage matching remain uncalibrated until our published audit; every receipt above links the matched article so you can check the match yourself).
  • Anything about outlets outside the AllSides-rated US spectrum — the circle construct is US-centric by its source.
